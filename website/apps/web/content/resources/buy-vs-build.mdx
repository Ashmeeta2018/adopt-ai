This is the most common question we're asked before a scoping call: "should we even be building something custom, or is there a tool we should just buy?"

It's a good question. The honest answer is: often, you should buy. This guide explains how to decide.

## The buy option

The buy option means subscribing to a SaaS tool that automates part of your workflow — Zapier, Make, a vertical-specific tool, or a platform like HubSpot that includes automation as a feature.

Buy is the right answer when:

- **The workflow is standard.** If a hundred other businesses your size have the same workflow, there's almost certainly a tool built for it. The workflow being "common" is a feature: the tool has been refined by thousands of edge cases you haven't encountered yet.
- **You want to move in days, not weeks.** A SaaS tool is live in hours. A custom agent takes two to eight weeks.
- **Your volume is low.** If you're processing fifty documents a month, a custom agent is overkill. The operational overhead alone isn't worth it.
- **You're not willing to own the infrastructure.** A custom agent runs in your cloud account. It needs monitoring, maintenance, and occasional retraining. If your team can't own that, buy.

The cost of the buy option is per-seat or per-task pricing. For low-volume, standard workflows, that's probably the right answer.

## When to build

Build is the right answer when one or more of the following are true:

**The workflow has non-standard inputs.** If your carrier emails, invoices, or support tickets don't look like what SaaS tools were trained on, a custom agent will outperform a generic one. The specificity is the point.

**The cost of an error is high.** Generic automation tools are designed for standard cases. When an edge case falls through, the behaviour is often undefined. A custom agent is designed around your error cases explicitly.

**The per-seat or per-task cost is significant at your volume.** If you're processing 10,000 items a month, the per-task fee on most SaaS tools starts to matter. Custom agents have a flat operate cost that doesn't scale with volume.

**You need the data to stay in your infrastructure.** Most SaaS automation tools send your data through their servers. For healthcare, financial services, or any company with a data residency requirement, that's often a blocker.

**You want to own the model.** A custom agent that's been fine-tuned on your data and your edge cases is a business asset. A SaaS subscription is a cost.

## The "neither" answer

Sometimes the right answer is to do neither — at least not yet.

**Do neither when the workflow isn't stable.** If the process changes every few months, you'll be rewriting the agent constantly. Wait for the process to stabilise before automating it.

**Do neither when the root problem is process, not volume.** If the workflow is slow because of unclear ownership or missing data, automating it will make it faster while preserving the chaos underneath. Fix the process first.

**Do neither when you don't have a review owner.** Every production agent needs someone who can review its output and provide feedback. If you don't have that person, the agent will drift over time and no one will notice.

## The decision framework

Ask these four questions in order:

1. **Is there a SaaS tool that handles this specific workflow for businesses our size?** If yes, evaluate it seriously before building anything.
2. **Is our workflow standard enough that the SaaS tool will handle our edge cases well?** If no, or if the answer requires months of configuration, build.
3. **Is our volume high enough that per-task pricing creates meaningful cost at maturity?** If yes, model out the cost comparison. Build often wins at scale.
4. **Can we own the infrastructure and the review cadence?** If no, buy even if build would be technically superior.

## The questions we ask clients

When a client comes to us having already decided to build, we still ask the buy question. The answer is sometimes "you've already evaluated this and build is right" — but sometimes it's "you assumed build was the answer because that's what you came here for."

There's no shame in the buy answer. We've told clients to use a tool rather than work with us. The right answer for the business is the right answer.
