This is the playbook we actually use. Every Spark and Lift engagement starts with the same process — and this document is that process, written down so you can follow it with or without us.

## What's inside

The playbook covers the full lifecycle of a production agent: from the two-week scoping sprint that determines whether to build at all, through the build and confidence-gate phases, to the 90-day operate period and eventual hand-off.

It is not a theoretical framework. Every section is based on a real engagement. Where we've made mistakes, we've written them in.

### Section 1: Is this agent worth building?

The decision criteria we use before starting any project. Covers:

- The return calculation (build cost ÷ weekly hours saved = payback in weeks)
- The seam analysis: where human judgment adds value vs where it doesn't
- The three categories of "we won't take this project": workflow too variable, data too thin, org not ready to escalate

### Section 2: The scoping sprint (weeks 1–2)

How to structure two weeks of discovery work that produces a build brief, not a requirements document. Includes the seven questions we ask in every customer session and the format of the one-page brief we produce at the end.

### Section 3: Building the agent

Architecture decisions for the three most common workflow types:
- Inbox triage and routing
- Document extraction and ledger posting
- Support classification and ticket creation

Includes the system prompt patterns we've found most reliable, the tool integration checklist, and the confidence-gate template.

### Section 4: The confidence gate in detail

The section most people want first. Covers:

- Why 0.5 is almost never the right threshold
- How to set the threshold as a business decision, not an engineering number
- Two-threshold and carrier-override patterns
- What to measure after you ship

### Section 5: The 90-day operate period

What we actually do during the operate phase, including:

- The three metrics we watch (volume, escalation rate, error rate)
- Alert thresholds that wake up a human vs. wait until morning
- The quarterly retraining cadence
- The runbook format we hand off at the end

### Section 6: The hand-off

What a complete hand-off package looks like. The runbook format, the credential documentation, the monitoring configuration, and the "one-year maintenance estimate" we include for every client.

## Who this is for

This playbook is most useful for:

- **Operations leaders** at 20–200-person businesses considering an AI automation investment. The return calculations and decision criteria in Section 1 will tell you whether a project is likely to make sense before you talk to anyone.
- **Technical leads** who've been asked to evaluate AI agent options. Sections 3 and 4 go into enough engineering detail to be useful without assuming deep ML background.
- **Agency or consultant founders** who want to understand how a fixed-price AI project is actually structured.

It is not useful for enterprise AI programmes with dedicated ML teams. If you have a model fine-tuning budget and a data science team, you're building something different from what we build.
